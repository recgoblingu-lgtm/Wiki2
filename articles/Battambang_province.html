Battambang province

Battambang Province is a province of Cambodia in the far northwest of the country. Bordering provinces are Banteay Meanchey to the north, Pursat to the east and south, Siem Reap to the northeast, and Pailin to the west. The northern and southern extremes of the province's western boundaries form part of the international border with Thailand. In addition, Tonlé Sap forms part of the northeastern boundary between Siem Reap and Pursat. Its capital and largest city is Battambang.

Background

Battambang Province ( Khmer : ខេត្តបាត់ដំបង , Khaêt Băt Dâmbâng ; [kʰaet ɓat ɗɑm.ɓɑːŋ] ) is a province of Cambodia in the far northwest of the country. Bordering provinces are Banteay Meanchey to the north, Pursat to the east and south, Siem Reap to the northeast, and Pailin to the west. The northern and southern extremes of the province's western boundaries form part of the international border with Thailand . In addition, Tonlé Sap forms part of the northeastern boundary between Siem Reap and Pursat. Its capital and largest city is Battambang . It is the fifth most populous province in Cambodia. In land area, Battambang is the fifth largest province of Cambodia. Battambang is one of the provinces included in the Tonle Sap Biosphere Reserve . The province's fertile rice fields have led to a mostly agricultural economy giving rise to the moniker "the rice bowl of Cambodia". The province features a range of cultures as well as natural resources. Seventy five percent of the area is jungles and mountains. The area has a tropical climate.

Etymology

The compound word, Battambang , in the title Battambang Province, comprises of the Khmer words băt ( បាត់ , 'lost') and dâmbâng ( ដំបង , 'stick'), which literally mean 'lost stick' in reference to the local legend of Lok Ta Dambang Kranhung ( លោកតាដំបងក្រញូង , Lok Ta Dâmbâng Krânhung ). During the annexation of Battambang Province to Siam , it was named Phra Tabong Province .

History

Battambang was annexed by both Siam and Cambodia from time to time because its location is in between both kingdoms. In 1769, Battambang and Siem Reap were conquered by King Taksin of Siam. On March 23, 1907, Battambang, with Siem Reap and Koh Kong, were annexed to French Indochina. During World War II it was annexed to Imperial Japan before being handed over to Thailand. After the war ended, it was returned to an independent Cambodia.

Administrative divisions

Battambang Province is divided into 13 districts and one municipality which are further subdivided into 93 communes ( Khmer : ឃុំ , khŭm ), 10 quarters ( Khmer : សង្កាត់ , sǎngkăt ) and 810 villages ( Khmer : ភូមិ , phum ). Local government entities within the province include the two towns and 12 subdistrict municipalities.
