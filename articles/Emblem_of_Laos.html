Background

The National Emblem of the Lao People's Democratic Republic shows the national shrine Pha That Luang . A dam is pictured, which is a symbol of power generation at the reservoir Nam Ngum . An asphalt street and a stylized watered field is pictured. In the lower part is a section of a gear wheel . The inscription on the left reads "Peace, Independence, Democracy" ( Lao script: ສັນຕິພາບ ເອກະລາດ ປະຊາທິປະໄຕ ) and on the right, "Unity and Prosperity" (Lao script: ເອກະພາບ ວັດຖະນາຖາວອນ ).

History

An example of socialist heraldry , the coat of arms was originally adopted in 1975. It was modified in August 1991 to deemphasize Communism as a reaction to the fall of the Soviet Union , at which time the red star and hammer and sickle were replaced with the national shrine at Pha That Luang . A description of the coat of arms is specified in the Laotian constitution: The National Emblem of the Lao People's Democratic Republic is a circle depicting in the bottom part one-half of a cog wheel and red ribbon with inscriptions [of the words] "Lao People's Democratic Republic", and [flanked by] crescent-shaped stalks of fully ripened rice at both sides and red ribbons bearing the inscription "Peace, Independence, Democracy, Unity, Prosperity". A picture of Pha That Luang Pagoda is located between the tips of the stalks of rice. A road, a paddy field , a forest and a hydroelectric dam are depicted in the middle of the circle. — Constitution of the Lao People's Democratic Republic, § 90
