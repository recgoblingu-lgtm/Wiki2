Background

A strong key is a naming convention in computer programming . Multiple components (such as DLLs) may share the same name while differing in version, a situation that can result in versioning conflicts. In the Microsoft .NET Framework , a strong key, also known as an SN key or strong name, is used to uniquely identify a component. This identification relies in part on public-key cryptography . Strong names ensure reliable binding between components, or between a root key and a component. However, they do not provide tamper resistance for the files containing those components. Strong naming is also used as a mechanism to mitigate issues commonly referred to as DLL hell . The key itself is generated by a separate program as a cryptographic key pair.
