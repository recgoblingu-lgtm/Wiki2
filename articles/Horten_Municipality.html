Background

Horten ( Norwegian pronunciation: [ˈhɔʈn̩] ; locally [ˈhɔʈːə] ) is a municipality in Vestfold county, Norway . It is located in the traditional district of Jarlsberg . The administrative centre of the municipality is the town of Horten . Other population centers in Horten Municipality include the town of Åsgårdstrand and the villages of Nykirke , Skoppum , and Borre . The municipality is located on a peninsula along the Ytre Oslofjord . The 70.96-square-kilometre (27.40 sq mi) municipality is the 341st largest by area out of the 357 municipalities in Norway. Horten Municipality is the 43rd most populous municipality in Norway with a population of 28,173. The municipality's population density is 407.7 inhabitants per square kilometre (1,056/sq mi) and its population has increased by 3.7% over the previous 10-year period. Borre National Park contains the largest known burial site in Scandinavia . It also has the largest collection of king's graves in Scandinavia. The local newspaper in Horten is named Gjengangeren , and covers mostly local news. The Bastøy Prison is located in the municipality on the island of Bastøy which is located about 3 kilometres (1.9 mi) offshore in the Ytre Oslofjord .

Administrative history

The formannskapsdistrikt law of 1837 created a system of local government in Norway by establishing each town ( ladested or kjøpstad ) and each Church of Norway prestegjeld as a civil municipality. On 28 September 1857, the small port village of Horten was designated as a ladested which meant it had to become a separate municipality. On 1 January 1858, the ladested of Horten was established as Horten Municipality. Initially, it had a population of 4,636. On 1 August 1921, an area of Borre Municipality (population: 287) was transferred into Horten Municipality. On 1 July 1951, another area of Borre Municipality (population: 308) was transferred into Horten Municipality. On 1 January 1986, the eastern Skavli area of Borre Municipality (population: 22) was transferred into Horten Municipality. On 1 January 1988, Borre Municipality (population: 9,098) was dissolved and merged with Horten Municipality (population: 12,994) to form a new, larger municipality named Borre (the name of this new municipality was later changed to Horten Municipality). Prior to the merger, Horten Municipality was the same as the town of Horten , but afterwards, the municipality was much larger than the town. The name of the new municipality was somewhat controversial and the government chose Borre over Horten for the new name. Name

The municipality (originally the town) is named after the old Horten farm (spelled "Hortan" in 1552) since the town was built on the old farm site. The name has an uncertain meaning. The name may come from a local dialect word hort which means the "outer, knotty bark on older trees" which likely derives from the Old Norse word hǫrtr which means "something uneven" or "wrinkled". Another option could be that it is derived from the Old Norse word hǫlkn or helkn which means "rocky ground", "shelter", or "place of refuge". yet another option is that it could be the definite form of hort which can mean "bulge" ( Old Norse : hǫrti ). Several hills and mountains in Norway have the name Horten or Horta and here it could be referring to the Brårudåsen hill lying in the middle of the town. From 1 January 1858 until 1 January 1988, the name was Horten Municipality . From 1 January 1988 until 1 June 2002, the name was Borre Municipality . Since 1 June 2002, the name has been Horten Municipality .

Coat of arms

The coat of arms was granted on 19 November 1907, just after Horten was designated as a kjøpstad , giving it town privileges . The blazon is "Per bend azure a fortress and boat on water and argent a rose" . This means the arms are divided with a diagonal line . The field (background) below the line shows a naval fort with a boat on the water. Above the line, there is a red rose and the field has a tincture of argent which means it is commonly colored white, but if it is made out of metal, then silver is used. The arms use the old "English"-style of shield with a mural crown sits on top signifying that the municipality contains a city. The design shows the importance of the Karljohansvern naval base and the gardens of the city. The arms were designed by Andreas Bloch . The municipal flag is white with the coat of arms in the centre.
