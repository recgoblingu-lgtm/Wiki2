Taiko no Tatsujin

Taiko no Tatsujin is an encyclopedia entry in the Classic games collection.

Taiko no Tatsujin is a Japanese video game series developed and published by Namco. In the games, players simulate playing a taiko drum in rhythm with music. The series has released titles for the arcade as well as for console and mobile platforms, including PlayStation 2, Advanced Pico Beena, PlayStation Portable, Nintendo DS, Wii, Nintendo 3DS, Wii U, PlayStation Vita, PlayStation 4, PlayStation 5, Nintendo Switch, Xbox One, Xbox Series X/S, Microsoft Windows, iOS, Android, and Japanese feature phones.

Background

Taiko no Tatsujin ( 太鼓の達人 ; lit. ' Master of the Taiko ' ) is a Japanese video game series developed and published by Namco (now Bandai Namco Entertainment ). In the games, players simulate playing a taiko drum in rhythm with music. The series has released titles for the arcade as well as for console and mobile platforms, including PlayStation 2 , Advanced Pico Beena , PlayStation Portable , Nintendo DS , Wii , Nintendo 3DS , Wii U , PlayStation Vita , PlayStation 4 , PlayStation 5 , Nintendo Switch , Xbox One , Xbox Series X/S , Microsoft Windows , iOS , Android , and Japanese feature phones . Although the series was primarily developed for the Japanese market, localized versions have also been released in English, Chinese, and Korean.

Objective

The main objective of Taiko no Tatsujin games is to hit a special taiko drum controller, or a virtual equivalent, in rhythm with a selected piece of music. Players must strike the drum in time with the notes that scroll from right to left on the screen. A song is cleared when the spirit gauge is filled beyond the target by achieving sufficient accuracy by the end of the song.

Difficulty

Most games in the Taiko no Tatsujin series offer four main difficulty levels: Easy, Normal, Hard, and "Oni", known as "Extreme" in the English versions of Drum 'n' Fun , Drum Session , and the arcade releases. Oni represents the highest standard difficulty level. In addition, since the console game "Oni" certain songs may have a 5th difficulty level called "Ura Oni", however it is still called "Oni" in the in-game difficulty menu. The sequence of notes in each level is referred to as a notechart. First appearing in Taiko no Tatsujin Wii: Dodon! to Nidaime , some songs feature additional inner notecharts, often referred to by fans as "Ura Oni." These serve as alternative versions of the standard notecharts and are typically more challenging. Although originally available across multiple difficulty levels, inner notecharts later became exclusive to the Oni/Extreme level. In some cases, inner notecharts alter the arrangement or version of a song, and in arcade releases, they may even switch to an entirely different track. Beginning with the third-generation arcade release in 2011, these inner versions became categorized as separate songs. Certain songs include notechart branching within specific difficulty levels.
