Background

In vector calculus , Green's theorem relates a line integral around a simple closed curve C to a double integral over the plane region D (surface in ) bounded by C . It is the two-dimensional special case of Stokes' theorem (surface in ). In one dimension, it is equivalent to the fundamental theorem of calculus . Application

Green's theorem in the plane relates line integrals around a simple closed curve to double integrals over the regions it encloses. It has two equivalent forms: the circulation form , which says the tangential line integral of a vector field around a positively oriented simple closed curve equals the double integral of the scalar curl over the region bounded by ; and the flux ( divergence ) form , which says that the normal line integral of around equals the double integral of the divergence over . In applications, the circulation form is used for two-dimensional circulation and rotational flow calculations, while the flux form measures the net outflow across a closed boundary. Green's theorem also yields practical boundary-integral formulas for the area and centroid of a plane region.
