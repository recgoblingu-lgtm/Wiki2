Background

Subway Builder is a 2026 transit simulation game by independent developer Colin Miller. The game is a single-player railroad construction and management simulator . Players build subway systems in real-world cities by constructing tracks and stations under realistic constraints and costs. They also design routes and manage operations to serve commuters simulated using real-world census data.

Gameplay

The player starts with a map of their selected city accurately populated with its road network, buildings and topographical features. The player builds subway stations and tracks in the city. Tracks can be built at different elevations to navigate the city and landscape. The player can build elevated and at grade tracks as well as tunnels at numerous depths. Three types of tracks and trains are available; heavy and light metro, and commuter rail. Heavy metro cost more to build and maintain than light. Heavy metro trains are based on the New York City Subway R211 stock, allowing up to 15 cars. Light metro trains are based on the Montreal REM Alstom Metropolis Saint-Laurent stock, allowing up to 4 cars. Commuter rail trains are based on the Long Island Rail Road M9 trains. Heavy and light metro are used to connect nearby areas with fast acceleration but a lower top speed, while commuter rail is used to connect area farther away with a low acceleration but higher top speed. The player can use various data analysis tools to see where residents and workers are concentrated and their daily commutes to plan the locations of stations and direction of routes. Once stations and tracks are built, the player then designs the routes that trains will travel on their subway system. Simulation

The game's simulation assigns generated commuters with home and work locations across a city map using a distance-based gravity model based on census data. The simulation then generates passenger flow and demand by making decisions for each commuter about the best way for them to travel based on travel time, cost and time of day. Commuter experience is also accounted for, with commuters discouraged by delayed or cancelled services. College student and airport commuters are also simulated with different travel behavior. There is a known problem where the simulated commuters prefer to avoid transfers, affecting travel time and commuter use.

Maps

There are currently 45 maps available as of August 2026 which include 44 cities available to play, and one sandbox map. 35 cities are from the United States, five from the United Kingdom, three from France, and one from Ireland: Additional maps are planned for future release in the coming months, both within and outside the U.S.
