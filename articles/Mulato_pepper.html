Background

The mulato pepper is the dried form of a variety of the poblano pepper. Mulatos are poblanos that ripen to a dark brown when fully mature. Other poblano varieties ripen to a red color, are harvested somewhat earlier, and when dried are called ancho peppers. The mulato is flat and wrinkled, and is always brownish-black in color. The average length and width of the mulato are 10 cm and 5 cm, respectively. Its shape is wide at the top, tapering to a blunt point. The mulato has been described as tasting somewhat like chocolate or licorice , with undertones of cherry and tobacco . Its heat rating is 2,500 to 3,000 on the Scoville scale .
