Lego Creator (video game)

Lego Creator (video game) is an encyclopedia entry in the Sandbox games collection.

Lego Creator is a sandbox game developed by Superscape and published by Lego Media for Windows. The game allows players to assemble virtual Lego bricks and Lego figurines to create scenes. It was one of a line-up of three titles released by the publisher for Christmas 1998, including Lego Loco and Lego Chess, to enter the software market. Upon release, Lego Creator received mixed reviews, with critics praising the concept, but faulting its user interface, visuals, and performance, and remarking that the game was not as satisfying to play with as its physical counterpart. Following release, three sequels in a Lego Creator series were released, including Lego Creator: Knights' Kingdom in 2000.

Background

Lego Creator is a sandbox game developed by Superscape and published by Lego Media for Windows . The game allows players to assemble virtual Lego bricks and Lego figurines to create scenes. It was one of a line-up of three titles released by the publisher for Christmas 1998, including Lego Loco and Lego Chess , to enter the software market. Upon release, Lego Creator received mixed reviews, with critics praising the concept, but faulting its user interface, visuals, and performance, and remarking that the game was not as satisfying to play with as its physical counterpart. Following release, three sequels in a Lego Creator series were released, including Lego Creator: Knights' Kingdom in 2000.

Gameplay

Lego Creator allows players to construct virtual scenes on a 3D plane with Lego bricks . Players navigate the interface using the computer mouse , and alternate between two modes: Build Mode and Play Mode. In Build Mode, players select and assemble scenes from an unlimited number of individual bricks, Lego figurines , structures, and accessories, or pre-made buildings and vehicles. Elements are selected by scrolling through a visual list of blocks. Players can assign custom actions to figures placed in the scene, including the sounds they make. A brick of dynamite , when built into models, causes it to blast into pieces. In Play Mode, figures and bricks placed in the scene animate, including Action Bricks, which play sound and lighting effects such as police sirens. The camera can be controlled in several ways. Players can control a free moving camera that can be adjusted for direction, altitude and speed. Cameras can also be built as pieces in the town allowing for players to see the scene in a fixed perspective. Figures and vehicles can also be selected to control them from their perspective.

Development and release

Lego Creator was published by Lego division Lego Media, established in 1996. Following the commercial success of Lego Island in 1997, Lego Media expanded its number of employees, and released Lego Creator as a joint launch title for the division's entry into the market for the Christmas 1998 season alongside Lego Loco and Lego Chess . Lego Creator was pitched as a flagship title to imitate play with the toy in a virtual space, with division managing director Mark Livingstone stating it was designed with no right, wrong, or prescribed way to play to reflect its design philosophy. According to executive producer Rob Smith, the game was partially inspired by earlier work of the company's Strategic Product Unit to digitise the toy using Silicon Graphics , although models were too detailed to implement into the game. These were recreated by London -based developer Superscape , who had experience building 3D graphics for virtual reality . Smith stated a key challenge of creating Lego Creator was working with Superscape and designer Guy Sunderland to design controls and a user interface that would be accessible to younger players, redesigning the interface through "multiple iterations" and extensively focus testing the game with children. Sales

Following audience research undertaken after the success of Lego Island , Lego expected Lego Creator to be a success and competitive with AAA games . Lego Creator was the highest-selling home education software title in North America in December 1998 according to PC Data , and the third-highest selling game in the United Kingdom in February 1999 according to ChartTrack and the ELSPA .
