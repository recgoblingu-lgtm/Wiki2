Geography of the Pitcairn Islands

Geography of the Pitcairn Islands is an encyclopedia entry in the Wikimedia discovery collection.

The Pitcairn Islands are a group of four islands in the south Pacific Ocean: the group comprises Pitcairn Island, Henderson Island, and two coral atolls, Oeno Island and Ducie Island.

Background

The Pitcairn Islands are a group of four islands in the south Pacific Ocean : the group comprises Pitcairn Island (a volcanic island ), Henderson Island (an uplifted coral island), and two coral atolls , Oeno Island and Ducie Island . The only inhabited island, Pitcairn, has an area of 5 km (1.9 sq mi) and a population density of 10/km (26/sq mi); it is only accessible by boat through Bounty Bay . The other islands are at a distance of more than 100 km (62 mi).

Location

The Pitcairn Islands as a group of islands in Oceania :( 25°04′00″S 130°05′00″W ﻿ / ﻿ 25.06667°S 130.08333°W ﻿ / -25.06667; -130.08333 ) Situated in the South Pacific Ocean , about one-half of the way from Peru to New Zealand , they are one of the most remote sites of human habitation on Earth. The only inhabited island, Pitcairn, is at 25.04 south, 130.06 west. Pitcairn is about 2,170 kilometres (1,350 mi) southeast of Tahiti , 5,310 kilometres (3,300 mi) from Auckland , New Zealand , and over 6,600 kilometres (4,100 mi) from Panama .

Area

Pitcairn Island is about 3.2 kilometres (2.0 mi) long and 1.6 kilometres (0.99 mi) wide. This is about 3 ⁄ 10 of the size of Washington, DC .

Maritime claims

Exclusive economic zone: 836,000 km (323,000 sq mi) Territorial sea: 3 nautical miles (5.6 km; 3.5 mi)
