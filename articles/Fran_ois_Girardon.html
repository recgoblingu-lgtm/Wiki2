François Girardon

Background

François Girardon ( French: [fʁɑ̃swa ʒiʁaʁdɔ̃] ; 17 March 1628 – 1 September 1715) was a French sculptor of the Louis XIV style or French Baroque , best known for his statues and busts of Louis XIV and for his statuary in the gardens of the Palace of Versailles .

Biography

He was born at Troyes . His father was a foundry worker. He was first trained as a joiner and woodcarver. His talent attracted the attention of the Chancellor of Louis XIV, Pierre Séguier , a serious patron of the arts, who arranged for him to work in the studio of François Anguier , and later, from 1648 to 1650 to live and apprentice in Rome. There he saw Baroque sculpture and met Bernini , but he came to reject that style and moved instead toward classicism and the models of ancient Roman sculpture. In 1650 he returned to France, and became a member of the group of artists, led by Charles Le Brun , the official painter of the King, and including the garden designer André Le Nôtre , who were commissioned to decorate the new royal park of the Chateau of Versailles . His principal contribution was the group of statuary representing Apollo served by the Nymphs , (1666–1675), symbolizing the Sun King himself, placed in a grotto close to the Palace. The figure of Apollo was inspired in form by the Apollo Belvedere of the Vatican, and featured two groups of figures; Apollo surrounded by nymphs , and a second group, next to the grotto, showing The Horses of the Sun being conducted to their royal stable.
