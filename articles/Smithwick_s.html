Background

Smithwick's ( / ˈ s m ɪ ð ɪ k s / ) is an Irish red ale -style beer . Smithwick's brewery was founded in Kilkenny in 1710 by John Smithwick and run by the Smithwick family of Kilkenny until 1965, when it was acquired by Guinness , now part of Diageo . The Kilkenny brewery was shut down in 2013 and production of all Smithwick's and Kilkenny branded beers moved to Dublin; parts of the old brewery were later converted into a "visitor experience".

History

Smithwick's Brewery was founded by John Smithwick in 1710. The brewery is on the site of a Franciscan abbey, where monks had brewed ale since the 14th century, and ruins of the original abbey still remain on its grounds. The old brewery was later renovated to become the " Smithwick's Experience Kilkenny " visitor attraction and centre. At the time of its closure, it was Ireland's oldest operating brewery. John Smithwick settled in Kilkenny. Shortly after his arrival, Smithwick went into the brewing business with Richard Cole on a piece of land that Cole had leased from the Duke of Ormond in 1705. Five years later, John Smithwick became the owner of the land. The brewery stayed small, servicing a loyal local following while John Smithwick diversified. Following John Smithwick's death, the brewery temporarily fell out of family hands. John Smithwick's great-grandson Edmond bought the brewery land back freehold and worked to reshape its future. Edmond concentrated on discovering new markets and successfully building export trade. Drinkers in England, Scotland and Wales developed a taste for Smithwick's brews and output increased fivefold. As a result of substantial contributions made to St Mary's Cathedral , Edmond became great friends with Irish liberal Daniel O'Connell , who later became godfather to one of his sons. Edmond Smithwick became well known and respected by the people

Smithwick Family

Smithwick Family The Smithwick family is one of Ireland’s historic brewing and mercantile families, with a legacy spanning more than 300 years. Established in the early eighteenth century, the family became prominent in Irish brewing, commerce, law and public life, producing several mayors of Kilkenny and distinguished members of the Irish judiciary. The family’s brewing dynasty dates to 1710 and became one of Ireland’s most recognisable brewing enterprises. During the nineteenth century, Edmond Smithwick, a four-time Mayor of Kilkenny and associate of Daniel O’Connell, played a significant role in the city’s commercial and civic development. The family’s historic residence was Kilcreene Lodge, Kilkenny. Walter and Eileen Smithwick were the parents of Judge Peter Smithwick, Michael, Anne, Judy, Paul and John. Judge Peter Smithwick (1937–2022) served as President of the Irish District Court from 1990 to 2005. One of Ireland’s prominent judicial figures, he subsequently chaired the Smithwick Tribunal, investigating alleged Garda collusion in the 1989 murders of two senior Royal Ulster Constabulary officers. He was also active in charitable and heritage organisations, including the Order of Malta. Sullivan's Brewery

In the Old Kilkenny Review , 1964, Peter Smithwick, K.M., Solicitor, wrote that the tradition in Kilkenny is that Sullivan's Brewery was founded in 1702 by Daniel Sullivan, a Protestant, who bought property in trust for Pierse Bryan of Jenkinstown , a Catholic who was prohibited by the Penal Laws from buying land. The property, on the West side of High Street, "standing backwards in James's Street", is believed to have been the site of Sullivan's Brewery, the forerunner of Smithwicks.
