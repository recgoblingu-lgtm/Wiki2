Paracosm

Paracosm is an encyclopedia entry in the Wikimedia discovery collection.

Background

A paracosm is a detailed imaginary world thought generally to originate in childhood. Their conception is spontaneous, and the creator of a paracosm has a complex and deeply felt relationship with this subjective universe, which may incorporate real-world or imaginary characters and conventions. This results in a very intimate but also often secret relationship with this world, meaning that few people ever know of its existence or contents. Commonly having its own geography, history, and language, it is an experience that is commonly developed during childhood, but becomes a rarer development with age. It continues over many months or even years, rarely lasting into adulthood. A paracosm can lead to the development of a heterocosm, which is a polished and edited version of a paracosm intended for sharing and publishing, such as some works of the Brontës.

Origin and usage

The concept was first described by Robert Silvey, with later research by British psychiatrist Stephen A. MacKeith and British psychologist David Cohen. In their questionnaire, they found that 93% of their respondents had created their paracosm by age 13. Besides the decreasing rate of creation with age, MacKeith and Silvey had also found that they rarely lasted into adulthood, with less than 10% of respondents reporting their paracosm to have survived into their adult life. The term "paracosm" was coined by Ben Vincent, a participant in Silvey's 1976 study and a self-professed paracosmist. Psychiatrists Delmont Morrison and Shirley Morrison mention paracosms and "paracosmic fantasy" in their book Memories of Loss and Dreams of Perfection , in the context of people who have suffered the death of a loved one or some other tragedy in childhood. For such people, paracosms function as a way of processing and understanding their early loss. They cite J. M. Barrie , Isak Dinesen and Emily Brontë as examples of people who created paracosms after the deaths of family members. Marjorie Taylor is another child development psychologist who explores paracosms as part of a study on imaginary friends .
