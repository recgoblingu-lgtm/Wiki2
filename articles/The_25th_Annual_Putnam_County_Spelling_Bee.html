Background

The 25th Annual Putnam County Spelling Bee is a one-act musical comedy with music and lyrics by William Finn and a book by Rachel Sheinkin, conceived by Rebecca Feldman with additional material by Jay Reiss. Based on Feldman's improvisational play C-R-E-P-U-S-C-U-L-E , it portrays a fictional spelling bee in which six quirky adolescents—played by adult actors—compete under the supervision of three equally offbeat grown-ups. Developed at the Barrington Stage Company in 2004, Spelling Bee opened off-Broadway at Second Stage Theater in February 2005 and transferred to Broadway 's Circle in the Square Theatre that May, directed by James Lapine and produced by David Stone with others. It played 1,136 performances before closing in January 2008 and was nominated for six Tony Awards , winning Best Book of a Musical (Sheinkin) and Best Featured Actor in a Musical ( Dan Fogler ). A distinctive feature of the show is that four real audience members are invited on stage to compete alongside the cast, while the official pronouncer—often an improv comedian —supplies absurd usage sentences on request. Some performances are presented as adults-only "Parent-Teacher Conferences", peppered with profanity and sexual references drawn from the cast's rehearsal ad-libs. Background and development

The musical originated in C-R-E-P-U-S-C-U-L-E , an improvisational play created by Rebecca Feldman and performed by The Farm, a New York improv-comedy troupe she founded. It was first staged in May 2002 at the Present Company Theatorium on the Lower East Side, with a second, expanded run that October. Even in that early, largely improvised form the piece already featured the three adult officials, several of the young spellers, and the audience-participation device. Sarah Saltzberg , who played the lisping speller Logainne and was then Wendy Wasserstein 's weekend nanny, appeared in the original production. Wasserstein, a friend of William Finn , saw the downtown show and brought it to his attention; at her suggestion Feldman and Jay Reiss showed Finn a video of the piece in September 2003. Finn, who said the material "screamed out to me 'musical'", brought in Rachel Sheinkin—his former student in New York University 's graduate musical-theatre writing program—and the two worked with Feldman to develop C-R-E-P-U-S-C-U-L-E into a full-length musical. Feldman retained a "conceived by" credit, Reiss was credited with additional material, and the finished show was billed as based on the original play. Off-Broadway (2005)

Spelling Bee began previews at the Second Stage Theater on January 11, 2005, and opened off-Broadway on February 7 to near-unanimous rave reviews. Ticket sales quickly passed $200,000, a box-office record for the company, and the limited run was extended before closing on March 20, 2005. James Lapine directed and Dan Knechtges choreographed, with a design team—retained for the Broadway transfer—of orchestrator Michael Starobin , music director Vadim Feichtner, scenic designer Beowulf Boritt , costume designer Jennifer Caprio, lighting designer Natasha Katz and sound designer Dan Moses Schreier . The production carried over its principal cast to Broadway.

Broadway (2005–2008)

The musical transferred to Broadway at the Circle in the Square Theatre , beginning previews on April 15, 2005, and opening on May 2, with Lapine and Knechtges again directing and choreographing. Capitalized at $3.5 million, Spelling Bee recouped its investment on September 4, 2005—about 18 weeks after opening—helped by weekly running costs of under $300,000 for its cast of nine and orchestra of five. It won the Tony Awards for Best Book (Rachel Sheinkin) and Best Featured Actor ( Dan Fogler ). The original principal cast departed gradually over 2005 and 2006, and the show was almost entirely recast on April 17, 2007—bringing in Jennifer Simard , James Monroe Iglehart , Greta Lee and others—before closing on January 20, 2008, after 1,136 performances and 21 previews and a cumulative Broadway gross of about $42.6 million.
