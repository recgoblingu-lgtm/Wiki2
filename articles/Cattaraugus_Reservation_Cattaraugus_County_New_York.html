Background

Cattaraugus Reservation ( Seneca : Ga’dä꞉gë́sgë꞉öʼ ) is an Indian reservation located partly in Cattaraugus County , New York , United States. The population was 314 at the 2010 census. The majority of the residents are of the federally recognized Seneca Nation , one of the original Five Nations of the Haudenosaunee Confederacy . The majority of the Cattaraugus Reservation is in Erie County and smaller portions are located in Cattaraugus County and Chautauqua County . The main population center in this portion of the reservation is Versailles ( Gasdë:gö:h ), which straddles the southern border of the reservation.

Geography

According to the United States Census Bureau , the Indian reservation has a total area of 6.0 mi (15.5 km). 5.8 mi (14.9 km) of it is land and 0.2 mi (0.6 km) of it (3.68%) is water. Cattaraugus Creek runs through the middle of the reservation, and Lake Erie forms part of the western boundary.

There were 136 housing units at an average density of 23.6/mi (9.1/km). The racial makeup of the Indian reservation was 4.38% White and 95.62% Native American . Hispanic or Latino of any race were 4.12% of the population. The average household size was 3.06 and the average family size was 3.52. In the Indian reservation the population was spread out, with 36.1% under the age of 18, 10.8% from 18 to 24, 31.2% from 25 to 44, 19.1% from 45 to 64, and 2.8% who were 65 years of age or older.
