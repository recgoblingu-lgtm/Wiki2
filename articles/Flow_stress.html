Background

In materials science , the flow stress , typically denoted as (or ), is defined as the instantaneous value of stress required to continue plastically deforming a material - to keep it flowing. It is most commonly, though not exclusively, used in reference to metals. On a stress-strain curve, the flow stress can be found anywhere within the plastic regime; more explicitly, a flow stress can be found for any value of strain between and including yield point ( ) and excluding fracture ( ): . The flow stress changes as deformation proceeds and usually increases as strain accumulates due to work hardening , although the flow stress could decrease due to any recovery process.
