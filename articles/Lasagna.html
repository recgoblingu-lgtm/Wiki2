Lasagna

Lasagna is a traditional layered pasta dish made with flat sheets of pasta, sauce, cheese, and various fillings such as meat, vegetables, or seafood.

It is widely associated with Italian cuisine, especially the region of Emilia-Romagna, but has become a popular comfort food around the world. Variations differ based on ingredients, dietary preferences, and cultural adaptations.

Background

Lasagna dates back to ancient times, with early forms of layered pasta appearing in Roman-era cookbooks. The modern version developed in Italy, where it became a staple baked dish combining pasta sheets with ragù, béchamel sauce, and cheese.

Over time, international versions of lasagna emerged, including vegetarian, white sauce, and seafood-based variations.

Preparation

Lasagna is typically prepared by layering pasta sheets with sauce and fillings, then baking it in an oven until the top becomes golden and the layers are fully cooked together.

Common ingredients include ground beef or pork, tomato sauce, ricotta or béchamel, mozzarella, and parmesan cheese.

Importance

Lasagna is considered a classic comfort food and is often served at family gatherings, holidays, and celebrations. It is valued for its rich flavor, versatility, and ability to be prepared in large portions.
