Background

Balne is a village and civil parish in the county of North Yorkshire in England, south of Selby . According to the 2001 census the parish had a population of 231, reducing to 224 at the 2011 census. The parish is bound to the north-east by the East Riding of Yorkshire and to the south by the Metropolitan Borough of Doncaster in South Yorkshire . Balne is part of the historic West Riding of Yorkshire . From 1974 to 2023 it was part of the Selby District , it is now administered by the unitary North Yorkshire Council . The name Balne possibly derives from the Middle English balne from the Latin balneum meaning ' bathing place'. The village formed the centre of the ancient district of Balne, which consisted of the eastern part of the Osgoldcross wapentake , probably including all the land between the River Don and the River Aire . Places such as Walden Stubbs and Fishlake were formerly described as being "-in-Balne", and Thorpe-in-Balne still is. Balne Croft Common near Thorne may have been a detached part of the township of Balne. The village has a Grade II Listed Building called Lowgate Farmhouse. Until 1958, Balne had a railway station as part of the North Eastern Railway.
