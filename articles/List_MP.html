Background

A list MP is a member of parliament (MP) elected from a party list rather than a geographic electoral district . The place in Parliament is due to the number of votes cast for the party, not to votes cast for the MP personally. This occurs only in countries with an electoral system based wholly or partly on party-list proportional representation .

Different systems

In closed list system , voters choose a party, the party receives a number of seats in Parliament proportional to its share of the vote, and the seats that party wins are filled by candidates in the order set by the party before the election. In open list systems , seats are also allocated to lists in proportion to their vote shares, but voters can express a preference for individual candidates and influence who fills those seats. The rules vary between systems, including how many candidates voters can support and how candidate preferences affect the order in which seats are filled. Such systems are used in Fiji , Indonesia and Sri Lanka . In other countries, a more complicated system is used. In the method used in Japan , South Korea and Taiwan , some seats are filled using party lists, while others are filled by the "traditional" first-past-the-post (FPP) voting system. Under the mixed member proportional (MMP) system, the method used in Germany and New Zealand , a merger of party-list representation and geographic representation is employed — parties contest geographic seats (districts) but are then "topped up" with members from a party list.

Indonesia

In Indonesia , members of the House of Representatives are elected through an open-list system in multi-member electoral districts. Voters can select either a political party or an individual candidate from a party’s list. Seats are allocated to parties proportionally according to their votes, with the candidates receiving the most individual votes within each party winning those seats. Unlike a closed-list system, the order in which candidates are elected is determined by voters rather than by the political parties.

Israel

Israel uses a closed list system to elect all 120 members of the Knesset in a single nationwide constituency, with seats allocated roughly in proportion to the vote shares of lists that meet the 3.25 per cent electoral threshold.
