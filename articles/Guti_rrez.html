Background

Gutiérrez ( UK : / ˌ ɡ ʊ t i ˈ ɛər ɛ z , - ə z / , US : / ˌ ɡ uː t i ˈ ɛər ə z , - ə s , ɡ uː ˈ t j ɛər ɛ s / , Spanish: [ɡuˈtjereθ] ) is a Spanish patronymic surname of Germanic origin, meaning "son of Gutier/Gutierre". Gutierre is an archaic Spanish form of Walter , now largely replaced with Gualterio . Gutiérrez is the Spanish form of the English surnames Walters , Watkins , and Watson , and has Germanic etymological origin. Today most bearers of the surname are Latin Americans of various origins. The Visigoths , who ruled Spain between the mid-5th and early 8th centuries, had a profound impact on the development of surnames. This name originally derived from the baptismal name Gutierre.
