Background

The BMW M51 is an inline-6 cylinder Diesel engine produced by the Upper Austrian BMW plant in Steyr from July 1991 through February 2000. Its predecessor is the BMW M21 ; the successor is the BMW M57 .

Description

The M51 is a water-cooled and turbocharged inline six-cylinder diesel engine. It is an indirect injection design with fuel is supplied by and electronically - controlled Bosch VP37 distribution injection pump. The displacement is 2,497 cc (152.4 cu in) and the compression ratio is 22.0:1. Some engine variants have an intercooler in addition to the turbocharger; vehicles so equipped can be identified by the designation tds . The M51 has a cast iron block and aluminum head, one chain driven overhead camshaft, and two valves per cylinder. Compared to the M21 the M51 features tappets and hydraulic valve lash adjustment. The fuel injection in the first engines and all E36 models is controlled by the Bosch DDE 2.1, which was replaced after the first technical revision by DDE 2.2, resulting in greater torque at lower revs. For lubrication, BMW originally approved a range of oil viscosities depending on the climate and ambient temperature, such was 10w-40 or 15w-40,l though 5w-40 synthetic oil is commonly used as modern replacement.
