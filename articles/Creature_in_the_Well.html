Background

Creature in the Well is a 2019 action-adventure game developed by Flight School Studio. It combines elements of dungeon crawlers , hack and slash games, puzzle games and pinball . The player controls BOT-C, a robot engineer attempting to restore an ancient weather machine beneath a mountain while contending with a large creature that has taken control of the facility. Rather than directly attacking enemies, the player charges and strikes energy orbs at machinery, hazards and targets positioned throughout the game's rooms. The game was principally created by Adam Volker and Bohdon Sayre. It developed from an experimental multiplayer prototype based around striking a ball between players before being reworked as a single-player adventure. Its visual design was influenced by comic artist Mike Mignola , while its mechanics drew from games including Rocket League , Breakout and Pong . Creature in the Well was released for Windows, Nintendo Switch and Xbox One in September 2019 and for PlayStation 4 in March 2020. The game received generally favorable reviews. Critics praised its unusual combination of genres, visual presentation and the variety produced by its tools and hand-designed rooms, while criticism focused on repetition, pacing, difficulty and its limited explanation of some mechanics. Gameplay

Creature in the Well is played from a top-down perspective and takes place primarily inside a dormant machine constructed within a mountain. The player controls BOT-C, the last functioning unit of a group of robot engineers created to maintain the facility. The machine was intended to protect the nearby town of Mirage from persistent sandstorms, but was disabled by the Creature inhabiting the mountain. The facility is divided into eight hand-designed dungeons containing interconnected rooms. Progress is based on producing energy by striking glowing balls into targets resembling pinball bumpers. Energy accumulated from these targets is used to unlock doors leading deeper into the facility. BOT-C carries two tools. One is used to gather, hold and charge balls, while a second strikes them toward targets. Additional tools found throughout the machine alter the behavior of the balls. These include equipment that displays their projected trajectories, divides a ball into several projectiles or modifies its electrical properties. Players can change their equipment according to the requirements of individual rooms. Challenges vary between slower puzzles based around angles and ricochets and faster encounters involving hazards such as cannons, lasers and hostile projectiles. Some rooms impose timing requirements, while others require the player to redirect balls fired by the environment

Development and release

Creature in the Well was principally created by Flight School Studio creative directors Adam Volker and Bohdon Sayre, who had worked together before joining the studio when it was established in 2017. Volker was responsible for much of the visual direction and story and contributed to level design, while Sayre worked on game design, programming, visual effects and other technical aspects. The project emerged from a process in which the developers created three prototypes before choosing one to develop further. Sayre had been playing Rocket League and the mobile game Ballz and became interested in building a game around bouncing a ball using an unconventional means of control. The resulting prototype allowed a character to strike a ball using a sword. The project initially began as a multiplayer game resembling air hockey , with players returning a ball to one another, before Volker and Sayre began designing puzzle rooms around the mechanic and shifted the project to single-player. Although the finished game was commonly described as pinball-inspired, the developers did not initially design it as a pinball game. Sayre cited Rocket League , Breakout and Pong among their references and said that another developer later pointed out the prototype's resemblance to pinball. Reception

According to review aggregator Metacritic , Creature in the Well received "generally favorable" reviews on all four platforms. OpenCritic reported an average score of 76 out of 100, with 58% of critics recommending the game. Jordan Ramée of GameSpot praised the way later rooms developed the game's central mechanic through hazards, timing challenges and different forms of ball manipulation. He preferred the faster encounters to some of its more conventional puzzles, writing that rapid movement and calculated ricochets could make combat resemble a hack-and-slash game despite the absence of conventional attacks. Lauren Morton of PC Gamer described Creature in the Well as combining pinball, dungeon crawling, puzzles and elements of bullet hell games. She liked the variety of its rooms and the ability to leave difficult optional challenges and return to them later. CJ Andriessen of Destructoid considered the game closer mechanically to Breakout than traditional pinball. He liked the interaction between the game's charging and striking tools and the experimentation encouraged by different equipment, but found that puzzle formats began repeating despite the game's relatively short length..
