Background

Municipalities ( Polish : Gmina [ˈɡmina] ; pl. gminy [ˈɡminɨ] ) are the basic unit of the administrative division of Poland . As of 1 January 2019, there were 2,479 municipalities throughout the country, encompassing over 43,000 villages. 940 municipalities include cities and towns, with 322 among them constituting an independent urban municipality ( Polish : gmina miejska ) consisting solely of a standalone town or one of the 107 cities, the latter governed by a city mayor ( prezydent miasta ). Municipalities have been the basic unit of territorial division in Poland since 1974, when it replaced the smaller gromada . Three or more municipalities make up a higher level unit called a county ( powiat ), except for those holding the status of a city with powiat rights . Each and every county has the seat in a city or town, in the latter case either an urban municipality or a part of an urban-rural municipality.

Types

There are three types of municipalities: Some rural municipalities have their seat in a town which itself is outside of the municipality's territory. For example, the rural Augustów Municipality is administered from the town of Augustów , but does not include the town, as Augustów is an urban type municipality in its own right. 107 urban municipalities constitute cities, distinguished from towns through being governed by a city mayor ( prezydent miasta ) instead of a town mayor ( burmistrz ), the status awarded automatically to all urban gminy over 100,000 inhabitants or those with a status of a city with powiat rights, with some others allowed to retain the earlier awarded title due to historical reasons. 66 among the 107 cities (including all voivodeship seats and all cities over 100,000 inhabitants) have the special status of city with powiat rights. Such a city exercises also powers and duties of a powiat while not belonging to any; nevertheless, it may still be a seat of a regular powiat, albeit without belonging to it administratively (such powiat thus being often "doughnut-shaped"). In such cities, the roles of the powiat organs are fulfilled by the ones of the urban gmina.

Government

Polish municipalities operate under a mayor-council government . The legislative and oversight body of each municipality is the elected municipal council ( rada gminy ), in an urban-rural municipality called the town and municipality council ( rada miasta i gminy ), while in an urban municipality it is called the town/city council ( rada miasta ). Any local laws considered non-compliant with the national ones may be invalidated by the respective voivode , whose rulings may be appealed to an administrative court. Decisions in individual cases may in turn be appealed to quasi-judicial bodies named local government boards of appeal [ pl ] , their ruling subject to appeal to an administrative court. Executive power is held in the municipality by a directly elected official, called wójt in rural municipality, a town mayor ( burmistrz ) in urban-rural and most urban municipalities which contain towns, or a city mayor ( prezydent miasta ) in the 107 urban municipalities containing cities, the status awarded automatically to all urban municipalities over 100,000 inhabitants or those with a status of a city with powiat rights, with some others allowed to retain the earlier awarded title due to historical reasons.
