Background

Andrzej Żuławski ( Polish: [ˈandʐɛj ʐuˈwafskʲi] ; 22 November 1940 – 17 February 2016) was a Polish filmmaker. His films, characterized by extreme emotional and physical intensity, largely rejected mainstream commercialism , polarized critics, and found their primary audience among European art-house viewers. Internationally, he is best known for his 1981 psychological horror film Possession . Born in Lwów under Soviet occupation , Żuławski was the son of writer and diplomat Mirosław Żuławski and a great-nephew of pioneering science-fiction author Jerzy Żuławski . He studied film in Paris , France during the late 1950s. Returning to Poland, he began his career as an assistant and second-unit director to Andrzej Wajda on films including Samson (1961), a segment of Love at Twenty (1962), and The Ashes (1965). After directing short television films, he made his feature debut with The Third Part of the Night (1971), co-written with his father and a hallucinatory, sensationalized retelling of their experiences during the Nazi occupation . His second feature, the allegorical horror The Devil (1972), was banned by Polish communist authorities for its perceived critique of the regime; Żuławski subsequently left for France. Early life

Andrzej Żuławski was born Andrei Miroslavovich Żuławski ( Polish : Andriej Mirosławowicz Żuławski ) on 22 November 1940 in Lwów (later Lviv), then in the Ukrainian Soviet Socialist Republic (Ukrainian SSR) of the Soviet Union. He used the name Andrzej, which is the Polish equivalent of Andrei; both are regional variants of the English name Andrew. His mother was named Czesława, and his father, Mirosław Żuławski (1913–1995), was a Polish civil servant (and later diplomat) who had studied and worked in Lviv while it was part of the Second Polish Republic and before its annexation by the Soviet Union in 1939. Through his father, Andrzej was a great-nephew of Jerzy Żuławski (1874–1915), author of the pioneering Polish science-fiction Lunar Trilogy , which Andrzej later adapted into the film On the Silver Globe . Andrzej had younger brothers Łukasz (b. 1947) and Mateusz (b. 1948) and a sister, Marta. During the German invasion of the Soviet Union in June 1941, while Lwów was under bombardment , Żuławski sustained serious injuries and lost the sight in one eye, while his sister Marta died. In 1945, after his father Mirosław was appointed cultural counselor at the Polish Embassy in Paris , Andrzej spent two years studying in France, where he experienced bullying from his peers. Career

Thanks to a recommendation from Roman Polanski , he obtained an assistant position with Andrzej Wajda (whose early films he greatly admired) on the production of Samson (1961). Żuławski also assisted Wajda on Love at Twenty (1962) and The Ashes (1965). Shortly afterward, in 1967, he directed two short television films: Pavoncello , based on a story by Stefan Żeromski , and The Song of Triumphant Love , adapted from Ivan Turgenev . Both films survive in color versions, although Polish Television originally broadcast them in black and white. His debut feature, The Third Part of the Night (1971) was co-written with his father Mirosław and was a sensationalized retelling of their experiences during World War II. When his second film The Devil was banned in Poland, he decided to move to France, where he made That Most Important Thing: Love (1975) with Romy Schneider . After returning to Poland he worked for two years on a film which the authorities did not allow him to finish ( On the Silver Globe ), based on a book by his great-uncle Jerzy. Since then he lived and worked mostly in France, making art films. Being described as a maverick who always defied mainstream commercialism, Żuławski enjoyed success mostly with the European art-house audiences. Personal life

Żuławski's first wife was Barbara Baranowska , a graphic artist associated with the Polish School of Posters . They met in 1962 on the set of Love at Twenty , while she was still married to writer Adolf Rudnicki , and wed in the late 1960s. During the marriage, he had affairs with Joanna Kasperska [ pl ] (lead of his 1967 TV short Pavoncello ) and Małgorzata Braunek (met while she was promoting Hunting Flies in 1969 and began during pre-production of his 1971 feature debut The Third Part of the Night ). Baranowska then learned of the affairs and filed for divorce. Baranowska later designed the French poster for Żuławski's film Possession (1981); Rudnicki also inspired an unused character in that film. Żuławski had three sons from different relationships. He married his second wife, Braunek, in 1971 after she divorced actor Janusz Guttner [ pl ] . Their son, Xawery , was born in December that year, and later became a film director.
