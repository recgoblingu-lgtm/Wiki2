Background

Thomas Bowles Shannon (September 21, 1827 – February 21, 1897) was an American merchant and politician who represented California in the U.S. House of Representatives from 1863 to 1865. He also several terms in the California State Legislature , including four months as speaker .

Early life

Shannon was born on September 21, 1827, in Westmoreland County, Pennsylvania . He attended the Westmoreland County public schools. He moved to Illinois in 1844 and by 1849 he moved during the California Gold Rush , to California. There he engaged in mining and mercantile pursuits in Plumas County in California.

Early political career

Shannon was originally a Whig in politics, but later supported the Free Soil wing of the Democratic Party , serving as a member of its California Central Committee in 1860. He later joined the Republican Party after the outbreak of the Civil War . He was a member of the California State Assembly in 1858, 1859, and 1861, and the California State Senate in 1862, representing Plumas County. He supported black testimony in the California courts in 1862 and 1863, a position the Democrats unsuccessfully used against him as an issue in his third campaign for the Assembly and his subsequent campaign for Congress.

House of Representatives

Running as a supporter of Senator John Conness , Shannon was elected as a Republican to the thirty-eighth Congress (March 4, 1863 – March 3, 1865), in which he served as chairman of the Committee on Expenditures in the Department of the Interior . As a congressman he participated little in debate, but voted consistently for administration measures. Due to political turmoil in California over Conness, Shannon was not a candidate for reelection in 1864. He was selected as a California Congressional delegate for the funeral of Abraham Lincoln , in which capacity he participated in speeches delivered outside the Ohio Statehouse in Columbus, Ohio .
