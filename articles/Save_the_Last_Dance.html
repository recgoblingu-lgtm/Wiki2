Background

Save the Last Dance is a 2001 American dance film , directed by Thomas Carter . It stars Julia Stiles and Sean Patrick Thomas as a teenage interracial couple in Chicago who work together to help Stiles' character train for a Juilliard School dance audition. It was produced by MTV Films . Save the Last Dance was released in the United States on January 12, 2001, by Paramount Pictures . It received mixed reviews from critics, and grossed $131.5 million on a $13 million budget. A direct-to-video sequel, Save the Last Dance 2 , was released in 2006.

Plot

Seventeen-year-old Sara Johnson, an aspiring ballet dancer in suburban Chicago , auditions for the Juilliard School after asking her mother to attend. Sara performs poorly and is rejected. Soon afterward, she learns that her mother was killed in a car accident while traveling to the audition. Blaming herself, Sara abandons ballet and moves to Chicago's South Side to live with her estranged father, Roy, a jazz trumpeter. Roy initially struggles to communicate with Sara, but they gradually begin rebuilding their relationship. Sara transfers to a majority-Black high school, where she befriends Chenille Reynolds, a teenage single mother. Chenille takes her to a nightclub called Stepps and introduces her to her brother Derek, an ambitious student who hopes to attend Georgetown University and become a pediatrician . After seeing Sara struggle with hip-hop dancing , Derek offers to teach her. The two begin practicing together, combining elements of ballet and hip hop. Derek takes Sara to see the Joffrey Ballet , prompting her to discuss her mother's death and her abandoned ambitions. Their friendship develops into a romantic relationship. At Stepps, however, a jealous Nikki interrupts them, making Sara retreat to the bar. Malakai, Derek's longtime friend, insults Sara by saying that there's no point on trying to be with him. Production

Julia Stiles landed the role of Sara after director Thomas Carter saw her dance scene in the 1999 film 10 Things I Hate About You . Lacking dance experience, Stiles prepared for her role by two months of intensive training for the ballet scenes and also rehearsing the choreography for the hip hop scenes. Fatima Robinson was the film's hip hop choreographer. Lemont High School in Lemont, IL was used as the filming location for Sara's suburban high school. Other locations around Lemont were also used.

Release

The film debuted at number 1 at the North American box office, making $27.5 million in its opening weekend. Though the film had a 44% decline in earnings the following weekend, it still held the top spot for another week. It grossed $91,057,006 in the US alone and $131.7 million worldwide.
