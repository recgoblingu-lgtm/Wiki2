Background

Quercus petraea , commonly known as the sessile oak , Welsh oak , Cornish oak , Irish oak or durmast oak , is a species of deciduous oak tree in the flowering plant family Fagaceae . It is native to most of Europe and into Anatolia and Iran . The sessile oak is the national tree of Ireland , and an unofficial emblem in Wales and Cornwall . Sessile oak is a significant source of Timber in Europe, and historically was important in ship construction. Most trees reach a height of 20-40 m, and can live for several centuries.

Description

The sessile oak is a large deciduous tree up to 40 metres (130 feet) tall, in the white oak section of the genus ( Quercus sect. Quercus ) and similar to the pedunculate oak ( Q. robur ), with which it overlaps extensively in range. The leaves are 7–14 centimetres ( 2 + 3 ⁄ 4 – 5 + 1 ⁄ 2 inches) long and 4–8 cm ( 1 + 1 ⁄ 2 –3 in) broad, evenly lobed with five to six lobes on each side and a 1 cm-long ( 1 ⁄ 2 in) petiole . Sessile oak is monoecious , with both male and female flowers found on the same tree. The female flowers are found on bracts . The male flowers are grouped into catkins , produced in the spring. The fruit is an acorn 2–3 cm ( 3 ⁄ 4 – 1 + 1 ⁄ 4 in) long and 1–2 cm ( 1 ⁄ 2 – 3 ⁄ 4 in) broad, which matures in about six months.

Comparison with pedunculate oak

Significant botanical differences from pedunculate oak ( Q. robur ) include the stalked leaves, and the stalkless (sessile) acorns from which one of its common names is derived. (With the pedunculate oak, it is the acorns which are pedunculate, i.e. on stalks, while the leaves are not.) It occurs in upland areas of altitudes over 300 m (984 ft) with higher rainfall and shallow, acidic, sandy soils. Its specific epithet petraea means "of rocky places". Q. robur , on the other hand, prefers finer, richer, and deeper soils at lower altitude. Fertile hybrids with Quercus robur named Quercus × rosacea are found wherever the two parent species occur and share or are intermediate in characters between the parents.

Distribution

Sessile oak is found throughout Europe, and is most common in upland areas with heavy soils (rocky and well draining) that is neutral to slightly acidic.
