Background

In statistics , canonical-correlation analysis ( CCA ), also called canonical variates analysis , is a way of inferring information from cross-covariance matrices . If we have two vectors X = ( X 1 , ..., X n ) and Y = ( Y 1 , ..., Y m ) of random variables , and there are correlations among the variables, then canonical-correlation analysis will find linear combinations of X and Y that have a maximum correlation with each other. T. R. Knapp notes that "virtually all of the commonly encountered parametric tests of significance can be treated as special cases of canonical-correlation analysis, which is the general procedure for investigating the relationships between two sets of variables." The method was first introduced by Harold Hotelling in 1936, although in the context of angles between flats the mathematical concept was published by Camille Jordan in 1875. CCA is important in multivariate statistics and multi-view learning, and a number of interpretations and extensions have been proposed, such as probabilistic CCA, sparse CCA, multi-view CCA, deep CCA, and DeepGeoCCA. Literature on the subject can on occasion be inconsistent with notation. These inconsistencies are noted in this article to help the reader make best use of the existing literature and techniques available.
