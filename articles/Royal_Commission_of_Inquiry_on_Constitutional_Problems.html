Background

The Royal Commission of Inquiry on Constitutional Problems or Tremblay Commission was called for by the premier of Quebec , Maurice Duplessis in 1953. The commission, chaired by Mr. Justice Thomas Tremblay , studied the problem of tax sharing between different levels of government and greater constitutional problems in Canada . The commission held 97 public meetings throughout Quebec, received 217 briefs, launched several studies, and finally published a five-volume report in 1956. It proposed a maximum level of taxation be established, the provincial responsibility for unemployment benefits, and a shared personal and corporate tax scheme between the federal and provincial governments.
