Background

The Western Xia or the Xi Xia ( Chinese : 西夏 ; pinyin : Xī Xià ; Wade–Giles : Hsi Hsia ), officially the Kingdom of the Great Xia of the White and Lofty ( Tangut : 𗴂𗹭𘜶𗴲𗂧 ) or Great Xia ( 大夏 ; Dà Xià ; Ta Hsia ), also known as the Tangut Empire , was a Tangut -led imperial dynasty of China that existed from 1038 to 1227 AD. At its peak, the dynasty ruled over 1,000,000 square kilometres (390,000 square miles) of modern-day north-central China. The capital of the Western Xia was Xingqing , now Yinchuan ; another major Xia city and archaeological site is Khara-Khoto . The Western Xia was annihilated by the Mongols in 1227 AD. Most of its written records and architecture were destroyed, and the origins and history of the empire remained obscure until Chinese and Western research in the 20th-century. Today the Tangut language and its unique script are extinct; only fragments of Tangut literature remain. The Western Xia occupied the area around the Hexi Corridor , a stretch of the Silk Road , the most important trade route between northern China and Central Asia . They made significant achievements in literature, art, music, and architecture, which was characterized as "shining and sparkling". State name

The full title of the Western Xia as named by their own state means, word for word, 'white', 'high', 'kingdom', 'great' ( 𗴂𗹭𗂧𘜶 , reconstructed as /*phiow¹-bjij²-lhjij-lhjij²/ ), or 'white', 'high', 'great', 'summer', 'kingdom' ( 𗴂𗹭𘜶𗴲𗂧 ). K.B. Kepping translates this as the "Great State of White and High". A corresponding Chinese name ( 白高大夏國 , "White High Great Xia State") was also used. Chinese and Japanese scholars commonly interpret the first two words as "upper reaches of the White River", which was possibly referring to the Yellow River . Kepping proposed the translation "the Kingdom of the Great Xia of the White and Lofty", and suggested that the name refers to a peak in the Helan Mountains named the "White and Lofty Mother". During the Western Xia period, the only Tangut language name used was the "Great State of White and High", while Chinese language sources in Western Xia used both the name Xia and the "White High Great Xia State". After the Western Xia was conquered by the Mongol Empire in 1227, Tangut language sources also started using the name Xia in addition to their original Tangut name. "Western Xia" or "Xi Xia" is a later historiographical term based on the state's Chinese name. Ethnonym

The region was known to the Tanguts and Tibetans as Minyak (reconstructed Tangut pronunciation: /mjɨ-njaa/ ). The Tanguts were called Mianyao or Miyao (彌藥) by the Chinese, mi-nyag (མི་ཉག་) by the Tibetans, and Mi-niah or Mi by the Tanguts themselves. The name Tangut ( Old Turkic : 𐱃𐰭𐰆𐱃 , romanized: Taŋut ) first appeared in the Orkhon inscriptions dated to 732-735, which is transcribed in Chinese as Tangwu or Tangute ( Tangghut ( Tangɣud ) in Mongolian ). Various forms of Tangut were used as a common name for certain tribes in the Amdo -Kokonor- Gansu region until the 19th century. The name Tangut was transmitted to Western explorers and writers, who passed on the term, making it the most common name in European, American, and Japanese scholarship.

Origins

The Tanguts originally came from the Qinghai - Sichuan - Tibet region, whose home originally was in the highlands of western Sichuan. According to Chinese records, which called them the Dangxiang, the Tanguts were descended from the Western Qiang people, and occupied the steppes around Qinghai Lake and the mountains to its south. In 608, the Tanguts helped the Sui dynasty defeat the Tuyuhun , however they were betrayed by the Sui forces, who took the chance to loot the Tanguts. In 635, they were requested to serve as guides for Emperor Taizong's campaign against Tuyuhun , but the Tang forces double crossed them in a surprise attack and seized thousands of livestock. In retaliation, the Tanguts attacked the Tang and killed thousands of their soldiers. By the 650s, the Tanguts had left their homeland to escape pressure from the Tibetans and migrated eastward, to what are now parts of Shanxi and Shaanxi . In 584-5 Tuoba Ningzong led the first group of Tanguts to submit to the Sui. In 628-629 another group under the leadership of Xifeng Bulai surrendered to the Tang. After the Tuyuhun were defeated in 635, the Tanguts under Tuoba Chici also surrendered. The 340,000 Tanguts were divided into 32 jimi prefectures under the control of Tangut chieftains appointed as prefects.
