Background

Lochem ( Dutch pronunciation: [ˈlɔxəm] ) is a city and municipality in the province of Gelderland in the Eastern Netherlands . In 2005, it merged with the municipality of Gorssel , retaining the name of Lochem. As of 2019, it had a population of 33,590.

Population centres

The city of Lochem is the municipality's main population centre. The villages of Barchem and Laren were already in the municipality of Lochem before 2005. Almen , Eefde (immediately north of Zutphen ), Epse , Harfsen and Gorssel itself were in the municipality of Gorssel until 2005, when they became part of the municipality of Lochem.

City of Lochem

Lochem, 18 km (11 mi) east of Zutphen , received city rights in 1233. Until the 17th century, it was often besieged and burnt down. After 1700, it became a small market town for local farmers. The village of Laren has a castle called Huis Verwolde, which has guided tours in the summer. A tree on its estate is said to be the thickest tree in the Netherlands ("de dikke boom van Verwolde").

Transport

Lochem is served by Lochem railway station , on the Zutphen–Glanerbeek railway .
