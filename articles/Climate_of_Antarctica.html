Background

The climate of Antarctica is the coldest and driest on Earth . Antarctica averages 166 mm (6.5 in) of precipitation per year and snow rarely melts on most parts of the continent. After Antarctic snow becomes compressed, it becomes the glacier ice that makes up the ice sheet . Weather fronts rarely penetrate far into the continent, because of the katabatic winds . Most of Antarctica has an ice-cap climate ( Köppen classification EF ) with very cold and dry weather.

Temperature

Temperatures in the Antarctic region vary with latitude, elevation, and distance from the ocean. East Antarctica is colder than West Antarctica , partly due to its higher elevation, while the Antarctic Peninsula has the most moderate climate. The highest temperatures occur in January along the coast, averaging slightly below freezing. The highest temperature ever recorded in the Antarctic region was 19.8 °C (67.6 °F) recorded at Signy Research Station , Signy Island on 30 January 1982, while the highest temperature on the continent of Antarctica was 18.3 °C (64.9 °F) at Esperanza Base on 6 February 2020. The lowest measured air temperature on Antarctica was set on 21 July 1983, when a temperature of −89.2 °C (−128.6 °F) was observed at Vostok Station at an elevation of 3,488 meters (11,444 feet). For comparison, this is 10.7 °C (19.3 °F) colder than subliming dry ice at standard temperature and pressure . Satellite measurements have identified even lower ground temperatures, with −93.2 °C (−135.8 °F) having been observed at the cloud-free Antarctic Plateau on 10 August 2010. Precipitation

The total precipitation on Antarctica, averaged over the entire continent, is about 166 millimetres (6.5 inches) per year (Vaughan et al., J. Clim. , 1999). The actual rates vary widely, from high values over the Peninsula (380 to 640 millimetres (15 to 25 inches) a year) to very low values (as little as 50 millimetres (2.0 inches) in the high interior (Bromwich, Reviews of Geophysics , 1988)). Areas that receive less than 250 millimetres (9.8 inches) of precipitation per year are classified as deserts . Almost all Antarctic precipitation falls as snow . Rainfall is rare and mainly occurs during the summer in coastal areas and surrounding islands. Note that the quoted precipitation is a measure of its equivalence to water, rather than being the actual depth of snow. The air in Antarctica is also very dry. The low temperatures result in a very low absolute humidity , which means that dry skin and cracked lips are a continual problem for scientists and expeditioners working on the continent.

Weather condition classification

The weather in Antarctica can be highly variable, and the weather conditions can often change dramatically in short periods of time. There are various classifications for describing weather conditions in Antarctica; restrictions given to workers during the different conditions vary by station and nation.
