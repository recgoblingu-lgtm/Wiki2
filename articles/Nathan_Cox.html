Background

Nathan "Karma" Cox is an American music video director and editor known for his work with rock, alternative metal, nu metal, and industrial rock artists. His directing credits include music videos for Linkin Park, System of a Down, Disturbed, Coal Chamber, Korn, Marilyn Manson, Queens of the Stone Age, Chevelle, and Suicide Silence. Cox's career became closely associated with the emergence and commercial popularity of nu metal during the late 1990s and early 2000s. Among his best-known works is Linkin Park's "In the End" (2001), which he co-directed with Joe Hahn and which won Best Rock Video at the 2002 MTV Video Music Awards. In addition to directing, Cox has worked extensively as an editor, with credits including music videos for Metallica, Foo Fighters, U2, and other recording artists.

Early career and the rise of nu metal (1990s–2000)

Cox established himself as a music video director during the late 1990s, working with emerging alternative metal and nu-metal acts. One of his early directing projects was Coal Chamber's "Loco" (1997), for which he received a Music Video Production Association nomination in the Directorial Debut category. In 1998, Cox directed and edited the music video for System of a Down's "Sugar", from the band's self-titled debut album. The video featured a combination of performance footage and satirical television-news imagery. It subsequently received a Billboard Music Video Award nomination for Best Hard Rock Video. Cox continued working with emerging metal acts, directing Disturbed's "Stupify" (2000), the lead single from the band's debut album, The Sickness . The video also received a Billboard Music Video Award nomination for Best Hard Rock Video.

Linkin Park and mainstream recognition (2001–2003)

In 2001, Cox collaborated with Linkin Park during the promotion of the band's debut album, Hybrid Theory . He co-directed the music videos for "Papercut" and "In the End" with band member Joe Hahn. The latter video combined live-action performances with computer-generated environments, presenting the band within a fantasy landscape featuring monumental architecture, animated vegetation, and changing weather. The production was filmed in Los Angeles during a break in the band's 2001 Ozzfest touring schedule. Its visual concept drew upon fantasy imagery and Art Nouveau influences. "In the End" won Best Rock Video at the 2002 MTV Video Music Awards and was also nominated for Video of the Year and Best Group Video. Cox additionally edited the music video for Linkin Park's "Points of Authority", using concert and touring footage. During this period, he continued his association with Disturbed, directing videos including "Down with the Sickness" and "Liberate", and worked on concert and documentary releases involving Disturbed and Korn.

Established rock and metal artists (2003–2010)

During the 2000s, Cox directed videos for an increasingly diverse group of rock and metal artists. His projects included Chevelle's "The Red", "Vitamin R (Leading Us Along)", and "The Clincher"; Marilyn Manson's "Personal Jesus"; Queens of the Stone Age's "Little Sister"; and Disturbed's "Stricken" and "Inside the Fire". Cox also directed Marilyn Manson's "Running to the Edge of the World" and Korn's "Let the Guilt Go". His editing work during this period included projects associated with Metallica and Foo Fighters. Metallica's "I Disappear" and Foo Fighters' "Best of You" received MTV Video Music Award nominations for Best Editing.
