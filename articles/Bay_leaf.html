Background

Bay leaves are aromatic leaves commonly used as herbs in cooking many world cuisines ; They come from various plants of species with distinctive flavours and fragrances as desired of those cuisines. The original English bay refers to the laurel ( Laurus nobilis ) common in Western especially Mediterranean cuisine . Other unrelated species yet dubbed as bay under similar applications include Umbellularia californica ("California bay laurel"), Cinnamomum tamala in Indian cuisine (hence "Indian bay leaf", or "tejpat"), Syzygium polyanthum (" Indonesian bay leaf"), Pimenta racemosa (" West Indian bay"), and Litsea glaucescens (" Mexican bay leaf"). Bay leaves contain essential oils, such as eucalyptol , terpenes , and methyleugenol , which contribute to their taste and aroma in soups, stews, meat, seafood, and vegetable dishes. These leaves are used whole either dried or fresh while cooking before taken out from dishes before consumption as they can be abrasive in the digestive tract ; these leaves are also less commonly used in ground form. Bay leaves are used as an insect repellent in pantries and as an active ingredient in killing jars for entomology . In Eastern Orthodoxy liturgy, Mediterranean bay laurel are used to symbolize Jesus' destruction of Hades and freeing of the dead. Chemical constituents

The leaves of the European / Mediterranean plant Laurus nobilis contain about 1.3% essential oils ( ol. lauri folii ), consisting of 45% eucalyptol , 12% other terpenes , 8–12% terpinyl acetate, 3–4% sesquiterpenes , 3% methyleugenol , and other α- and β- pinenes , phellandrene , linalool , geraniol , terpineol , and also contain lauric acid .

Taste and aroma

If eaten whole, Laurus nobilis bay leaves are pungent and have a sharp, bitter taste. As with many spices and flavourings, the fragrance of the bay leaf is more noticeable than its taste. When the leaf is dried, the aroma is herbal, slightly floral, and somewhat similar to oregano and thyme . Myrcene , a component of many essential oils used in perfumery, can be extracted from this bay leaf. They also contain eugenol . Food writer J. Kenji Lopez-Alt advises boiling bay leaves in a pot of water then tasting the water at five minutes and at an hour to be able to more accurately determine their flavour.

Culinary

Bay leaves especially Mediterranean bay is typically used whole in cooking to flavor broths , grains , soups , stews and stocks . All leaves are typically removed before serving as they can be abrasive in the digestive tract. . In Indian cuisine, Western bay leaves are sometimes used in place of Indian bay/tej leaf ( tezpattā ( तेज़पत्ता , in Hindi), Tejpātā (তেজপাতা) in Bengali, তেজ পাত in Assamese and usually rendered into English as Tej Patta), although they have a different flavour. They are most often used in rice dishes like biryani and as an ingredient in garam masala . Mediterranean bay leaves were used for flavouring by ancient Greeks. They are a fixture in the cooking of many European cuisines (particularly those of the Mediterranean ), as well as in the Americas. They are used in soups, stews, brines, meat, seafood, vegetable dishes, and sauces. The leaves also flavour many classic French and Italian dishes. The leaves are most often used whole (sometimes in a bouquet garni ) and removed before serving. In the Philippines, dried bay laurel leaves as introduced by the Spanish have been integrated in several Filipino dishes, such as menudo , beef pares , and adobo . Thai and Laotian cuisine employ bay leaf ( Thai : ใบกระวาน , bai kra wān ) in a few Arab-influenced dishes, notably massaman curry .
