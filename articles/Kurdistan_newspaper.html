Background

Kurdistan was the first Kurdish newspaper. It was first published on April 22, 1898 in Cairo , Egypt by Mikdad Midhat Bedir Khan , a member of the Kürdistan Teali Cemiyeti . In four years, 31 issues were printed in cities as Cairo, Geneva , London and Folkestone . It was an opposition newspaper published in exile (outside the Ottoman Empire ) and supported by the Committee of Union and Progress and was published in Constantinople after the Young Turk revolution in 1908. It was often printed at CUP linked print shops which caused the newspaper to move to where ever the CUP had to establish itself anew due to the CUP's persecution by Ottoman ambassadors loyal to Sultan Abdülhamid . In 1914, during World War I , the newspaper returned to Cairo. Its first issues were published solely in Kurmanji, but from the 4th issue some texts in Turkish were also published. The newspaper is described as a nationalist newspaper as it made notions of territorial entity Kurdistan. In its folio, it was mentioned to be distributed for free within the borders of Kurdistan, while in the exterior its cost was 80 pennies. Beginning with the second issue onwards, it published a series of the Kurdish national epic Mem u Zin . From its 6th issue the newspaper was published in Geneva, and edited by Abdurrahman Bedir Khan , Mikdads brother who had close ties to Abdullah Cevdet and İshak Sükuti , both editors
